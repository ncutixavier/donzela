A cycle has four phases. Hormones rise and fall in a steady pattern, so you may notice different feelings and energy levels at different times.

## 1. Menstrual phase (your period)
Days 1 to about 5. The lining of the womb is shed. You may feel cramps, tiredness or a need to rest. Iron needs are higher while you bleed.

## 2. Follicular phase
From the end of the period until ovulation. Oestrogen rises, the womb lining rebuilds, and many women feel more energy and focus.

## 3. Ovulation
Usually about 14 days before the next period, though this varies. One egg is released. You may notice clear, slippery discharge, a little mid-cycle pain on one side, or a slight rise in body temperature.

## 4. Luteal phase
From ovulation until the next period, usually 12 to 14 days. Progesterone rises. Some women feel bloated, tender breasts, mood changes or cravings. These symptoms, often called PMS, are common. If they stop your daily life, talk to a health worker.

## Using this in Donzela
Your daily log lets you record how you feel in each phase. Over a few months, you may see patterns that help you plan rest, exercise and work.
