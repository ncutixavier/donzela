Emergency contraception is for after sex that was not protected, or when a method failed, for example a condom broke or pills were missed. It is **not** a regular method.

## Options
- **Emergency pill (levonorgestrel):** works best within **72 hours**, and can still work up to **5 days** (120 hours) after sex. Works better the sooner it is taken.
- **Copper IUD:** a health worker can insert it within **5 days**. It is the most effective emergency option and can also be kept as a long-term method.
- **Ulipristal pill:** a prescription option that can work up to 5 days, ask a health worker.

## Where to get it
- Health centres, and pharmacies in Rwanda, can advise you.
- Do not wait. The sooner you act, the better it works.

## After taking it
- Your next period may come early or late. Log it in Donzela.
- Nausea, headache or tiredness can happen. Tell a health worker if you vomit within 3 hours of taking the pill.
- It does not protect against infections. Get tested if you are worried.

## Not sure you are pregnant?
Take a pregnancy test about 3 weeks after unprotected sex, or sooner if you have a late period.
