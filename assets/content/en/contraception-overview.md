Contraception lets you decide if and when to have children. Cycle tracking alone is **not** reliable contraception. Choose a method with a health worker who can help you pick what suits you.

## Long-acting methods (most effective, no daily action)
- **Implant:** a small rod under the skin of the arm, works for several years.
- **IUD (coil):** a small device placed in the womb. Copper IUDs work for many years and do not use hormones.

## Hormonal methods
- **Injection:** given every 1 to 3 months, depending on the type.
- **Pills:** taken every day at the same time. Missing pills lowers protection.

## Barrier methods
- **Male condom:** one of the few methods that also protects against HIV and other sexually transmitted infections. Use a new one for each sex act.
- **Female condom:** inserted before sex, also protects against infections.

## Permanent methods
- Tubal ligation or vasectomy. These are permanent; talk it through carefully first.

## Things to know
- Ask for a method that suits your health history and how often you need it.
- Long-acting methods can be removed or stopped when you want to conceive.
- Some methods can cause spotting or mood changes at first. These often ease.
- Services are offered at health centres. Ask the staff what is available near you.

If you think you may be pregnant, do a pregnancy test before starting a new method.
